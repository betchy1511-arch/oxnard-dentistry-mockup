Dental Implants in Oxnard, CA: Replace Missing Teeth for Good

A missing tooth changes small things first. You chew on the other side. You smile with your mouth closed. Over time, the jawbone under the gap begins to shrink, and neighboring teeth start drifting toward the space.

Dental implants stop all of that. A small titanium post takes the place of the missing root, your bone bonds to it, and a custom crown finishes the job. At Oxnard Dentistry, Dr. Kourosh Keihani, DDS, plans every implant on 3D imaging and gives you an honest answer about whether an implant is truly your best option.

Before anything else

Are Implants Right for You? An Honest Answer First

Implants aren't automatically the answer for every missing tooth, and a consultation here starts with that honesty. Three things decide your candidacy:

Gums

Your gum health comes first

Active gum disease has to be treated before implant surgery, because implants need healthy gums the same way teeth do. Treating it first protects your investment.

Bone

Bone loss usually has a solution

If your jawbone has shrunk since losing the tooth, that rarely rules you out; it usually just adds a step. Bone grafting rebuilds the foundation, and we'll show you on your 3D scan exactly what's needed and why.

Fit

Sometimes another option genuinely fits better

Health conditions, budget, or timeline can make a bridge or a well-made denture the smarter choice for you, and we'd rather tell you that than sell you an implant. Our guide to whether dental implants are really your better choice walks through the honest comparison.

Just had a tooth pulled? Timing matters too: our guide to healing after a tooth extraction explains the window between extraction and implant placement.

Treatment options

Your Implant Options, Explained

Best for One missing tooth

Single Tooth Implant

One post, one crown, one restored tooth. Unlike a bridge, a single implant doesn't touch the healthy teeth on either side, and it keeps the bone under the gap alive and stimulated. This is the option most patients picture when they think of implants, and for one missing tooth it's usually the gold standard.

Best for Most or all teeth in an arch

All-on-4® Full Arch Replacement

When most or all of the teeth in an arch are failing, All-on-4® replaces the entire row on just four strategically angled implants. The new teeth are fixed in place: no adhesive, no soaking overnight, no denture slipping mid-sentence. Many patients who assumed they were headed for traditional dentures qualify, even with some bone loss, because the implant angles take advantage of the strongest available bone.

Best for Three or four missing teeth in a row

Implant-Supported Bridges

Missing three or four teeth in a row? Instead of one implant per tooth, an implant-supported bridge anchors a row of replacement teeth on two implants. You get fixed, natural-feeling teeth at a lower cost than individual implants for every gap.

Best for You already wear a denture

Implant-Retained Dentures

If you already wear a denture, a few implants can change your daily life more than you'd expect. The denture snaps onto the implants: it stays put while you eat and talk, no paste required, and the implants preserve the jawbone that traditional dentures slowly wear down. It unclips for cleaning, so the routine stays familiar.

Building the foundation

Bone Grafting and Sinus Lifts, When You Need a Foundation First

Jawbone shrinks when a tooth has been missing for a while, and some upper back teeth sit close to the sinus. Neither has to end your implant plans.

Bone Grafting

Bone grafting rebuilds thickness and height in the jaw, giving the implant solid anchorage. Depending on the case, grafting happens months before implant placement or during the same visit.

Sinus Lift

A sinus lift gently raises the sinus floor and adds bone beneath it, creating room for implants in the upper back jaw.

Both are routine, and both are planned on your 3D scan. If your scan shows you need one, we'll show you the image, explain the why, and price it into your written quote so nothing is sprung on you later.

Start to finish

How the Implant Process Works, Start to Finish

  1. 1

    Consultation and 3D Scan

    We examine your mouth, take the cone beam scan, and map your treatment, including any gum therapy or grafting needed first. You leave with a written plan and quote.

  2. 2

    Implant Placement

    The post is placed in the jaw under local anesthetic, with comfort options available for anxious patients. Soreness afterward typically peaks within a few days and responds to over-the-counter pain relief.

  3. 33 to 6 months

    Healing and Integration

    Over the next three to six months, your bone fuses to the implant. It's the least eventful step and the most important one. In select cases, a temporary tooth can be placed the same day, so you don't leave with a gap.

  4. 4

    The Final Restoration

    A custom crown, bridge, or denture attachment is fitted, matched to your natural teeth, and secured. Then you go back to eating, talking, and smiling without thinking about it.

Start to finish, expect roughly three to eight months depending on healing and any preparatory work. Slower done right beats fast and redone.
